How Much Does a Teleprompter Cost in New York? 2026 Rates

6 min read • Updated November 2026

You need a teleprompter for a shoot or event in New York and the first question is almost always the same: what will it cost? Here are the ranges we actually quote in 2026, what moves a job toward the top or bottom of each range, and every line item you will see on your estimate.

NYC and Westchester Premium

The half-day rate in Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens and Westchester runs $800–$1,000—higher than the rest of the state because operating costs in the city are steeper. Garage fees near Midtown venues, bridge and tunnel tolls, and tight freight-elevator windows at buildings like One World Trade or the Marriott Marquis all factor in. Upstate jobs in Albany, Rochester, Syracuse or Buffalo start at $600–$800 for a half day. Full-day and equipment-inclusive packages carry the same statewide range.

What Changes the Price

Equipment complexity. A single camera-mount prompter for a two-person interview in a Chelsea studio costs less than a dual presidential glass setup with confidence monitors for a keynote at the Javits Center or Lincoln Center.

Multi-day discount. Three or more consecutive days take 10% off the day rate. Week-long sales kickoffs at Midtown conference hotels benefit automatically.

Rush booking. Under 48 hours’ notice adds 50%. Most Manhattan corporate events give two to three weeks’ lead time, which avoids the rush fee entirely.

Travel. Travel is its own line on the quote: IRS standard mileage round trip from the assigned operator’s base. For Manhattan jobs the travel line is usually just parking or a garage, billed at cost. Upstate events or Hudson Valley shoots show the full mileage.

What Is Included

  • An experienced operator who loads, formats and scrolls your script live, matching each speaker’s pace
  • Equipment setup and testing before call time or doors
  • A rehearsal pass with every speaker so they feel the scroll speed before it counts
  • Real-time script edits when someone rewrites a paragraph on the way to the venue
  • Coordination with your house AV crew or production company on monitors, switching and signal flow

Manhattan Logistics Worth Knowing

Midtown hotels and convention centers often enforce tight load-in windows, require freight-elevator reservations and follow union labor rules for rigging. None of that changes the operator rate, but it can affect how early the crew needs to arrive. If your venue is the Javits Center, a Financial District office tower or a hotel ballroom near Times Square, send us the address with your quote request and we will confirm load-in details with the building before your date.

Brooklyn and Queens venues—Barclays Center, Industry City event spaces, studios in Greenpoint—generally have easier drive-in access and lower parking costs, which keeps the travel line smaller.

How Booking Works

  1. Send the date, venue, number of speakers and the type of setup you need.
  2. We reply with a written quote listing operator, equipment, travel and overtime terms as separate lines.
  3. A 50% deposit holds the date. The balance is invoiced after the event.

Most quotes go out the same business day. If you are comparing vendors, our line-item format makes apples-to-apples comparison straightforward.

Ways to Keep Costs Down

  • Book early. Two weeks’ notice avoids the rush fee. Allow three to four weeks during busy fall conference season.
  • Bundle days. A three-day annual meeting earns the multi-day discount automatically.
  • Send a near-final script early. Less reformatting on site means less time on the clock.
  • Tell us what your AV company already provides. If they supply monitors or switching, we do not duplicate gear you already have.
